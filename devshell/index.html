    Measure the drawings. Count the work. Price the job.

    1. Open plans (or drop them here). They open on this computer; nothing is uploaded.
    2. Give each sheet a scale: pick a Preset, or Calibrate on a dimension you know.
    3. Make an item under Items (linear, area or count), then click on the sheet to take it off.
    4. Add costs and an assembly to the item; the quantities and the job total below update as you go.
    5. Save takeoff keeps everything in a project file that reopens on top of the same PDFs.

          Show lengths as

          How lengths read on the sheet, in the panels and in the status bar. Follow Global uses the metric length unit you starred in Settings › Global › Units (feet-inches when none is starred). Only the display changes: quantities, costs and exports are worked out from the exact lengths.

          Show temperatures as

          How an item's temperature reads in the panels, and what a bare number means when you type one. Follow Global uses the temperature unit you starred in Settings › Global › Units (°C when lengths are metric, °F otherwise). Only the display changes: the value is kept exactly.

          Feet and inches

          How lengths read on the sheet, in the panels and in the status bar. Only the display is rounded: quantities, costs and exports are worked out from the exact lengths (exports show 1/16").

          Currency symbol

          The symbol shown before prices, in the tables, the bid and the exports. It only changes how amounts are written: nothing is converted, so type your rates in this currency.

          New items

          Every item you make from now on starts with this waste. You can still change it per item, and items you already have keep theirs.

          Auto count

          Where the Match slider is when a search finishes. Lower finds more look-alikes for you to reject; higher offers fewer. 75% is the start.

          Drawing

          Door openings and gaps up to this wide count as closed when the Room tool fills a room. Wider than your widest door lets a room run into the next one.

          A drawing's lines often stop a hair short of each other. The Path tool joins lines whose ends are closer than this. 1.5 is a good start; [ and ] change it while you pick.

          While you measure or calibrate, a point near a line of the PDF jumps to its end, its midpoint, a crossing, or the nearest point on it, and a marker shows which. F3 or Snap in the status bar turns it on and off. Scanned sheets have no lines to snap to.

          Runs, outlines and cut-outs then go at 0°, 45°, 90° or 135° from the last point; hold Shift to click at any angle. Arc points and circles are never snapped.

          Corner indicator

          In the 3D check view only. The view cube sits at the top right: click a face, edge or corner to look from there, drag it to orbit, drag the compass ring to turn. The axis icon is a small X, Y, Z marker at the bottom left. The flat sheet has neither.

          From add-ons and files

          Ticked: the layout you pick above is kept for this app alone, and the other apps keep theirs. Unticked: it follows the choice for every app. In SuperCad Studio the layout follows the app you are working in.

          Your own layouts

          A layout is a .ccworkspace.json file: data only, read as untrusted. It arranges the same building blocks in every app; each app fills them with its own tools. Drop a .ccworkspace.json file here to import it.

          Work environments

          Work environments. Save your whole setup under a name (the layout, menu style and keys, toolbar, panels and My tools, for every app) and switch between setups. The theme and the language stay as they are.

          If something went missing

          Lost a toolbar, a menu or a panel?

          Puts the toolbar (order, lock and buttons), the panels, the screen mode and the menu style back to how TakeoffCraft starts. Your theme, language, sizes and TakeoffCraft's own settings stay as they are.

          Loading the units…

          A file keeps its own unit (a drawing's units, a project's feet or meters), and that unit stays in its list even when it is turned off here.

          Hardware specs

          Sharing your hardware specs allows us to build more efficient engines. The information is aggregated and not associated with your account: the graphics card, processor cores, memory and screen size, the browser and system family, and the app — no files, no name, no address.

          Full-screen crosshair

          Sizes and panels

          Text and spacing

          The bars, toolbars, panels and dialogs of TakeoffCraft, bigger or smaller; the drawing keeps its own zoom. Panel text size works on top of it.

          Compact tightens the row heights, panel padding, toolbar buttons and panel text of TakeoffCraft, so more fits on the screen. A touch screen keeps its large targets either way.

          Panels

          You can also drag a panel's inner edge. Double-click the edge to put it back.

          Panel widths apply when the panels sit beside the drawing (wider windows).

          Panels

          Drag a panel by its name to dock it left, right or at the bottom, or drop it over the drawing to float it. A new window can go on your other screen. Reset layout puts every panel back where it started.

          Behaviour

          In a panel with several sections, opening one (by clicking its heading or by choosing a tool in the menu) closes the others so the panel stays on what you are doing. The lock beside a heading keeps that section open. Turn this off to open and close sections yourself.

          Add-ons

            Add-ons add features to TakeoffCraft. Switching one off takes effect at once; some need a reload to go completely.

            Developer mode is on: add-ons that are not signed can load. Only use add-ons you trust.
